01

The problem

Quick commerce lives on price perception, but every rupee of discount comes straight out of a thin margin. Prices were set by hand across thousands of SKUs and several dark stores, so they drifted: some items sat above competitors and lost orders, others were priced below cost.

02

The insight

Pricing is not one decision but a stack of rules with an order of priority. Cost sets the floor, the MRP sets the ceiling, competitors set the reference, and a few manual calls override everything. Encode the order once and every store gets a consistent, defensible price.

03

What I built

A dual-track engine in Google Sheets, one track for packaged goods and one for fresh produce, covering every dark store. It reads cost, competitor and stock data, applies the rule stack and outputs clean, store-level upload files every pricing cycle.

04

How the engine decides a price

01 · INPUT

Data

Weighted average cost, competitor prices, MRP, stock by store

02 · CANDIDATES

Four prices

Cost ceiling, competitor −₹1, intelligence-adjusted, normal margin

03 · CHOOSE

Lowest wins

The engine takes the minimum candidate

04 · GUARDRAILS

Validate

Price floor → cost +₹1 → overrides → MRP cap → round up

05 · OUTPUT

Upload

One row per SKU per store, ready for the catalogue

THE CORE IDEA

Compute several candidate prices (cost ceiling, competitor-matched, intelligence-adjusted, normal margin), take the lowest, then pass it through guardrails in a fixed order so no price ever breaks a floor, the MRP or a manual decision.
